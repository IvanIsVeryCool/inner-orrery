    Reading the sky

    Four regions of space

    Every feeling is placed on two dimensions psychologists use to map emotion: valence, from unpleasant to pleasant, and arousal, from calm to intense. Together they form Russell's circumplex model of affect. Each quarter of it is a different place in space. Open the Star map to see every entry plotted on those two axes, then travel to a region.

    The Horizon
    Intense and unpleasant. A black hole.
    Ember Nebula
    Intense and pleasant. A stellar nursery lit by a young cluster.
    Tidepool

    The Drift
    Calm and unpleasant. A planetary nebula, the shell a dying star leaves behind.

    The seven parameters

    Label, bodily signature, trigger, context, history and baseline, failure mode, and regulation. Each log walks through all seven; only the label is required.

    The physics is real

    The black hole is ray-traced through the Schwarzschild metric. Light bends around it, the far side of the disk wraps over the top, the side turning toward you glows brighter and bluer, and stars behind it are lensed into arcs. The wide sky's bending is softened a little so the background stays readable.
